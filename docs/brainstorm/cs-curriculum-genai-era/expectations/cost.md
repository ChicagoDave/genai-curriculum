**Time box**: unbudgeted, exercised in every decision record (was unplaced; settled 2026-09-27), plus one scripted slice where cost decides

**Dived**: 2026-09-27

Roster candidate 16. Dived out of roster order because six consecutive dives handed it a data point: a matrix row (option evaluation), a design factor on every kind choice (persistence), the row where the professor's override most often lands (0006), the spend behind every emitted line (operability), the price of redundancy (resilience), and the penalty for building for a scale nobody stated (performance). The dive found those were two different things being called one. Settled 2026-09-27: **there are two cost levers.**

## What the corporate acceptor holds the graduate to

**Two levers**, each with a standard side and a process side (decision college-0007):

1. **Project cost: the resources it takes to build and change the thing.** People's time, the calendar, and, in this era, the agent's spend: the tokens and runs the accuracy cycle consumes on the way to an accepted slice. The standard side is the company's delivery capacity and its rules about it: a cohort has these people for this many slices, and an agent budget per slice the professor sets in the guardrail. The process side is the stakeholder's: what the business wants by when, recorded in the business-side delivery timelines the product owner owns, and what it will give up to get it. The last dive's early-building penalty is this lever: a cache for forty bookings a day spent project cost on nothing the business asked for.
2. **Operational cost: what it takes to run the thing.** Cloud or on-prem, the stores, the telemetry retained, the redundancy, the second region. The standard side is the platform the professor fixed (decision college-0003 already accepted the platform's cost as the classroom's) and the approved products in the guardrail, which are approved partly because they are already paid for. The process side is the stakeholder's: whether a turned-away patient is worth a queue, whether the January rush is worth a second region, how long telemetry is kept.

**The acceptor states both**, and the two trade against each other. Building the read model now is project cost now against operational cost later. Deferring it is the reverse. A graduate who can only state one lever will always choose the other without knowing it.

**Where cost enters from above the scenario**: the professor's override in decision college-0006. A stakeholder who re-weights a cost row is the company's budget speaking through the scenario, and the cohort meets a priority it did not derive.

## What the graduate must be able to state

- **Both levers, as rows in every matrix** (decision college-0009). Project cost and operational cost are separate weighted rows, each traced to a business-side file: the delivery timeline for the first, a stated run-cost tolerance or the platform's price for the second.
- **An operational estimate for a kind choice.** Per month, in round numbers, from the approved product's pricing: what this store, this retention, this redundancy costs at the stated volume. Not precise; defensible.
- **A project estimate for a scope.** In slices and people, against the delivery timeline: what this scenario costs to accept, and what it costs to change later if deferred. The product owner role states it; the developer role challenges it (decision college-0008).
- **The trade between the levers, named.** When a record chooses project cost now to save operational cost later, or the reverse, it says so and says at what number the choice flips.
- **What a deferral costs.** The cheapest option in a matrix is often "not yet," and it has a cost row of its own: the scope it pushes past the timeline, and the operational cost of running without it.

## What proves it in a slice

- **A matrix with two cost rows, weighted from two files.** Any decision record with more than one candidate. The slice passes when the project row traces to the delivery timeline and the operational row to a stated tolerance or price, and the two are not the same number in different clothes.
- **The professor overrides a cost weight.** The scripted slice from decision college-0006, now pinned to this dive: the override lands on one lever and flips the result. The slice passes when the cohort re-totals, does not re-score, and states which lever the company just spoke through.
- **The growth event's record.** From the performance dive: the measure that the new number justifies carries both rows, the project cost of building it now and the operational cost of running it, and the record says why it was not built three slices earlier.
- **A scope that does not fit the timeline.** The product owner role pushes back on a stakeholder request because the project cost exceeds the delivery timeline, unprompted (decision college-0008). The slice passes when the push-back is recorded and the stakeholder either narrows the scope or moves the date.
- **The agent's spend, stated.** A slice whose accuracy cycle ran long. The slice passes when the record states what the extra runs cost against the agent budget and what refinement would have saved them. This is the era's new line item and the cohort meets it early.

## Collisions

- **Option evaluation (candidate 19) and decision college-0006**: cost is two rows in the matrix, not one. Decision college-0009 amends 0006 to say so.
- **Data persistence and change**: the operational lever on every kind choice; the project lever on every migration.
- **Performance and scale**: the growth event is where both levers are largest at once, and the early-building penalty is the project lever by name.
- **Resilience**: redundancy and queues are the largest operational rows the business will weight; the manual fallback is a project-cost-zero option with an operational cost paid in the receptionist's time.
- **Operability**: retention and alert volume are operational rows; the runbook is a project row.
- **Change management (candidate 15)**: the project lever's home for the cost of changing what already shipped. That dive decides whether rollout cost is stated there or here.
- **Scenarios / requirements / workflows**: the delivery timeline is a business-side file (README cohort-system table) and the project lever's source of weights.
- **Role cycling (decision college-0002)**: the product owner states project cost and the developer challenges it; the reverse for operational cost, where the developer estimates and the product owner challenges. Both roles hold both levers.
- **Decision college-0008**: cost push-back is the most common push-back a real stakeholder meets, and the professor does not invite it.
- **Stack guardrail (decision college-0005)**: the agent budget per slice joins the guardrail on the project side; the platform and approved products already carry the operational side. Recorded as an addition below.

## Membership verdict

**Expectation, two levers.** The acceptor states a project budget and an operational tolerance and holds the graduate to knowing both and trading them knowingly. It is exercised as a **skill** inside every decision record rather than in a unit of its own, the way option evaluation is, which is why it carries no time box. There is no named external discipline; the matrix is the form.

## Expansion budget

**None of its own.** Cost is two rows in slice 1's first matrix and in every matrix after it. The scripted override slice (decision college-0006) is where it decides an outcome. The agent-spend line item arrives the first time an accuracy cycle runs long enough to notice.
